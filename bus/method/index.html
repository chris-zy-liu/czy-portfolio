Field notes / 01 Apr–Sep 2026 · Independent project

From bus positions
to arrival estimates.

A transit prediction system built around two constraints: arrival times had to be inferred from noisy GPS traces, and the training pipeline had to fit on a 1 GB Linux VPS.

I owned collection, ETL, feature engineering, modeling, the interface, and deployment.

01 / The data problem

A position is
not an arrival.

The collector polled Georgia Tech’s public transit API every 15 seconds and stored positions in SQLite. The May 4–September 21 UTC history contained 3,471,196 observations, but no independently verified arrival timestamps.

I split trajectories on vehicle or route changes and gaps longer than five minutes. Within each continuous segment, route stop order, a 60-meter proximity threshold, and one-stop lookahead were used to infer arrivals. Labels were positive times to the next inferred arrival, capped at one hour and confined to the same segment.

02 / The engineering constraint

Millions of records.
One gigabyte.

I used binary search over arrival timestamps instead of repeated linear scans, alongside deterministic sampling and bounded-memory processing. The pipeline processed the recorded history without loading the entire labeled dataset into memory for training.

The estimator is scikit-learn’s HistGradientBoostingRegressor, using absolute-error loss and native categorical route and stop inputs. Its 14 inputs describe position, motion, time, and the next stop.

Position & motion
Latitude, longitude, speed, heading
Time
Hour, minute, day of week, weekend indicator
Stop context
Distance to next stop, seconds since last stop, next-stop latitude and longitude
Identity
Route ID, next-stop ID

03 / The evaluation

Test forward
in time.

The evaluation candidate was trained on 350,000 earlier examples and scored on 100,000 examples from September 15–21. A one-hour separation before the holdout kept training arrival labels from extending into the evaluation period.

Half of evaluated predictions were within roughly 23 seconds of the inferred arrival. The larger RMSE indicates a tail of substantially larger errors.

What these results establish

These are next-stop errors against GPS-inferred labels, not independently observed arrival times. After evaluation, the deployed estimator was refitted on 450,000 sampled examples through September 21, including the holdout period. The table describes the pre-refit candidate; it is not an independent test of the final deployed artifact.

Subsequent-stop estimates extend the next-stop prediction with segment medians. Their accuracy has not been separately established.

04 / From model to interface

A working system
you can inspect.

A Python HTTP API serves the model to a MapLibre transit dashboard. SQLite retains the historical records, and systemd manages the services on the Linux VPS.

Live positions and predictions refresh every 15 seconds. History recording is paused; live viewing and access to saved trips remain available.
